Its flag, eight gold stars on a blue sky, had been designed years earlier by a 13-year-old boy living in an orphanage. And overnight,  Texas stopped being the biggest state in America. Alaskans still like to remind Texans, "Behave or we'll split in half and make you number three." 5 years later, on Good Friday 1964,  the ground began to shake.

Magnitude 9.2, 2. The most powerful earthquake ever recorded in North America. It lasted more than 4 minutes. Entire streets dropped into the earth. The waves it created reached as far as California. Alaska rebuilt.

Curious. And 4 years later, it found out what it was really sitting on. Oil. In 1968, at Prudo Bay on the frozen northern coast, drillers hit the largest oil field in North America. To get it out, America built a pipeline 800 m long across three mountain ranges and hundreds of rivers.

More than half of it stands on stilts so its heat doesn't melt the frozen ground underneath. It wasn't all good news. In 1989, a tanker called the Exxon Vald ran a ground and spilled 11 million gallons of oil into Alaskan waters.

But the oil also paid for something no other state had ever  tried. Alaska put part of the money into a fund for its people. Today, that fund is worth more than $80 billion. And every year, almost every Alaskan, every man,  woman, and child receives a check just for living there.

Today, Alaska is still like nowhere else. Fewer than a million people in a land  bigger than most countries. Most of its towns can't be reached by road, and it has more pilots per person than any other state.

Because for many Alaskans, a small plane is the family car. In its northernmost town, the sun doesn't set for almost  3 months in summer and doesn't rise for 2 months in winter. So, let's go back to that deal.

7.2 million. In today's money, around $150 million. In  return, America got gold, oil worth hundreds of billions, a fortress facing Asia, and a land bigger than Texas, California, and Montana combined.
