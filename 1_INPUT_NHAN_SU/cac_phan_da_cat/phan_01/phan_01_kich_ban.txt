Imagine selling your house for $7 and then finding out there was a gold mine in the basement.  That is exactly what Russia did. Except the house was Alaska and the basement had gold, oil, and a few hundred billion dollar.

The price 2 cents an acre. And the  funny part, Americans thought they were the ones getting robbed. They called it an ice box, a frozen wasteland, a polar bear garden. They had no idea they had just made  one of the greatest deals in history.

This is the story of Alaska. And it starts long before Russia, long before America. It starts with a bridge that no longer exists. More than 15,000 years ago, the oceans were lower. And between Asia and North America, there was land, a bridge of grass and ice, hundreds of miles wide.

People walked across it, following the animals they hunted. Surprised, they were the first humans to ever set foot in the Americas. Some kept moving south. Others stayed. They became the  Inupiat, the Eupic, the Yunongan, the Tlingit, the Aabaskan.

They even gave the land its name. Alaska comes from a native word that means  the great land. For thousands of years, it belonged to them and the rest of the world didn't know it existed. Then in 1741, a ship appeared.

Its captain was Vitus Bearing, a Danish explorer working for the Russian Empire. His mission, find out what lies east of Siberia. He found Alaska. Bearing never made it home. He died on the way back, shipwrecked on a frozen island.

But his crew survived the winter, built a new boat from the wreck, and sailed home with something that changed everything. Sea otter fur. The softest, thickest fur in the world. In China, a single pelt was worth a fortune.

Russians called it soft gold. And the rush was on. Serious. Russian hunters poured across the sea island by island. They forced the native Unangan people to hunt for them. Thousands died  from violence and disease.
